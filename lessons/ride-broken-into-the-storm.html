Ride Broken, Into the Storm

April 27 – May 9, 2024 · Waynesboro, Tennessee → Metropolis, Illinois · Posted from the road on Day 1,296 · 66,769.6 documented miles — this tour

RIDE BROKEN — Chapter One: Tennessee, the film, 5:57 · also on the Ride Or Die America Page

April 27, 2024. One year into the ride, he rolled north out of Waynesboro, Tennessee, toward Kentucky — and everything started breaking at once. Two spokes snapped the same day; four by Grand Rivers, the tire walking side to side. The trailer hung on hose clamps, zip ties, and JB Weld. The seat broke. His sandals broke. Then his boots broke. He was out of money.

At a little church in Only, Tennessee, he stopped for water and said it out loud: “Everything’s broke and I’m broke.”

He camped a night at Loretta Lynn’s Ranch — the ranch stood empty, and that night he watched her granddaughter sing on American Idol, on TV, right there. On May 2 he woke at Compassion Church, where he had slept on holy ground. On May 4, outside Dover, the angels came back — with bacon and eggs on a grill.

INTO THE STORM — Chapter Two: Kentucky, the film, 2:50 · also on the Ride Or Die America Page

May 5–9: the Land Between the Lakes — 187 miles of it, by his count — and the spokes kept popping. In Grand Rivers, Jackie, the owner of Wood-N-Wave, switched out his whole rig to a one-by-nine with a granny gear. Beast Mode.

One night he weathered the warnings zipped inside his tent on a ridge. On May 8, at First Choice Farm & Lawn in Calvert City, the crew fed them in their garage while tornadoes were touching down in the area — then drove him down to Paducah, where Roadie got a new Burley trailer. He gave the old InStep to a homeless man outside the bike shop: he needed a home on wheels more than Coach needed a spare.

“I’d rather be on trail and die on trail than anything in this world and that’s for sure.”

Both chapters carry their own original duet, written for this film: “RIDE BROKEN” for Chapter One and “INTO THE STORM” for Chapter Two. The man’s voice is where you’ve been — broke, sore, held together with clamps. The woman’s voice is where you’re going. The chorus is both of them agreeing on one thing:

“I ride broken, I ride on. I ride into the storm.”

The lesson — THE DISCIPLINE: Two moves today, one from each chapter. Fix one broken thing with what’s already in your house — and take one step into the storm you’ve been avoiding.

R.O.D.A. — Ride Out Depleted Anyway.
