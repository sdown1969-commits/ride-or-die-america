Flip the Switch

Aug 1 – Sep 24, 2024 · Montana → Northern Idaho · Posted from the road on Day 1,298 · 66,769.6 documented miles — this tour

YOU DON'T HAVE FOREVER — Film #6 Chapter Four, the film, 5:09 · also on the Ride Or Die America Page

August 1, 2024. He rolled out of Yellowstone country into Montana and straight into a furnace — a hundred degrees by day, thirty-nine by morning, in pain from the day before, preaching to the camera with a sunburned face. At the bike camp in Twin Bridges he slept in a little cabin he called the Taj Mahal, and every afternoon he ran the same routine: words in his ears, then preaching them back to himself until they stuck.

You know this stretch of your own life. The alarm goes off and the negotiation starts — just rest, just quit, just wait for a better day. That voice sounds like it’s protecting you. It isn’t. It’s spending the only thing you can never earn back: time. And the clock doesn’t negotiate back.

The road kept feeding him angels the whole way north: a fed night and a front porch in Ravalli, a hot tub at Flathead Lake, a cabin behind a stranger’s house, a pop-up trailer in Kalispell, and Gwen, who came to his campfire with steaks and cooked them over the flames. He pointed at Going-to-the-Sun Road and let it go — no time, passes waiting, a coastline to reach. Then the posts stop for twenty-eight days: a recovery under a Montana roof, a quiet camp on the Lochsa River, the Idaho line on August 22, rail trails through the pines in 32-degree mornings — and on September 22, one post: frighteningly close to death, and still riding.

What carried him through all of it wasn’t motivation. It was the routine. The switch is not a mood — it’s a practice. Every afternoon in camp, he listens to something motivational, inspirational, faith-based — and then he preaches it back to himself, out loud, until it sticks. His words: “I gotta program my mind like nobody’s business every single day.” Your mind is getting programmed either way — by the day, by the pain, by the voice that says quit — or by you. Pick who’s holding the pen.

The chapter carries its own original duet, written for this film: “FLIP THE SWITCH.” The man’s voice carries the fall, the doubt, the get-back-up commands. The woman’s voice carries the dawn, the worthiness, the belief. The chorus is both of them together, and it is the line this chapter leaves in your hands:

“Flip the switch — you don’t have forever.”

The lesson — THE DISCIPLINE: Pick your line — one sentence that tells you the truth when the day sucks. Say it out loud, like you mean it. Then do the first rep: one push-up, one page, one mile, one call you’ve been avoiding. Line first. Rep second. It takes under five minutes, tonight or tomorrow — and it compounds.

R.O.D.A. — Ride Out Depleted Anyway.
