Just Gotta Do It, Purpose in the Pain

May 11 – June 20, 2024 · Metropolis, Illinois → Tribune, Kansas · Posted from the road on Day 1,296 · 66,769.6 documented miles — this tour

JUST GOTTA DO IT — Chapter One: Illinois Into Misery, the film, 6:08 · also on the Ride Or Die America Page

May 11, 2024. At a rest stop in Karnak, Illinois, an old lady prayed over his bike and trailer, said she’d be back — and came back and gave him $100. He never asked her why. He just thanked God and rode on: through the Tunnel Hill tunnel in the dark, ice cream on a porch in Vienna, a camp at Sandburn Junction where the sign says WELCOME BIKERS.

At Chester he stood over the Mississippi with Missouri on the far bank and brutal wind on the water. In Farmington he took a zero day at Al’s Place — you always take a zero day at Al’s Place. Then Missouri started climbing: roller-coaster hills, three hundred pounds of rig, close to a heart attack every day.

And from May 23 to May 27, there is nothing. No photo, no video, no post. Five days of just surviving it. His words for it: “That’s why I go silent sometimes. I don’t need a camera to prove it — I just gotta do it.”

PURPOSE IN THE PAIN — Chapter Two: Kansas, the film, 4:34 · also on the Ride Or Die America Page

June 2, in Hepler, Kansas: a man named Dale Nimmo put his hands on him and worked two states of misery out. At dawn on June 4 he stood in front of a white church you can see for miles and posted four words’ worth of testimony: “Restored, healed, and ready to ride.”

Then Kansas turned the heat on. June 13: fifty-seven miles into 105 degrees, real feel 112, a storm on the horizon — and under the park shelter in La Crosse he prayed the prayer nobody wants to pray: use me, use this pain, show me the purpose and how I can serve — and if that’s not going to happen, just take me now.

The method after that: cemeteries for shade — make it easy for the Lord to take you — and a goat-track golf course to douse yourself in water. And on a hill in Kansas, a sign he has passed for years: CHRIST PILOT ME. “That sign rides with me everywhere. And so does he.”

June 20, under the shelter in Tribune, two days out from the Colorado line, he told Kansas what he thought of it — the wind that never stops, the biting flies, the heat with no forgiveness in it — and finished the sentence the way he finishes every sentence: “But I ride or I die.” That is where this film ends. The road runs west. That’s the next film.

Both chapters carry their own original duet, written for this film: “JUST GOTTA DO IT” for Chapter One and “PURPOSE IN THE PAIN” for Chapter Two. The man’s voice is the grind and the fire. The woman’s voice is the belief and the dawn after. The choruses are both of them agreeing on one thing each:

“I don’t need a camera to prove it — I just gotta do it.” · “There’s gotta be purpose in the pain.”

The lesson — THE DISCIPLINE: Two moves today, one from each chapter. Do one thing that counts with no witness and no post — and take the pain you’ve been asking God to remove and give it a job: one useful thing, done inside it, for somebody else.

R.O.D.A. — Ride Out Depleted Anyway.
