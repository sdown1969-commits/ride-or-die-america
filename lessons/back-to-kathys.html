BACK TO KATHY’S — the film, 5:55 · also on Coach’s personal profile

February 6, 2024. Two months off the bike, he rolled out of Lloyd, Florida riding weak. Kathy Rothman and her husband opened the door — that day, and again on the way back west. In between: the whole Florida coast, and a prayer the road answered with weight.

A 34-degree night under the palmettos at Goose Pasture. A cabin out back at Jack’s Cycles in Perry. Strangers kept saying yes. Then Fanning Springs — out of water, the park on a do-not-drink notice — and Ranger Michelle Passino-Benson came back with her husband, a trailer, toys, food — and Roadie.

He was asking for strength and endurance and more power. He added about 35, 40 pounds to his gear and rig — and learning how to pull all that gave him the strength. First campfire. First dog park. Fifty-something miles a day, out like a light.

In Dunnellon, Cathy Hill and Kirk Roggenkamp — a cyclists’ garage, a dog passed around like family. On February 24, the Atlantic: a biker dude pulled up and offered his lunch, and at the pier he said it out loud — at least Virginia Beach. Probably Maine. The longest day ever ended at a prayer box on the side of the road, and the road bent back to Kathy’s.

The film carries its own original song, “WORTH THE WEIGHT” — built on his own words from this leg: the prayer for strength, and his line about the puppy — he’s heavy, but he’s worth it. The chorus carries the whole doctrine of the comeback: “He’s worth the weight.”

The lesson — Ask for strength, and the road may not lighten your load. It may add forty pounds to it — and learning to pull that weight is the strength you asked for. Getting stronger, harder, faster, longer, every day.
